# B6 terminal UI: the Scout terminal in the sandbox

Synthetic data: invented players and clubs in the Regista League, generated by Regista.

**Where it is.** It is the Terminal section under Scout, Maya Lindqvist's dashboard (`#scout/terminal`). The section
markup is in `index.html`, the code in `terminal.js`, and the styles in `terminal.css`. `scout.js` gained one change:
`'terminal'` in its section list. `terminal.json` (7.6 MB) loads only when the section opens for the first time.
Clips play from `terminal.json`'s `app` (`http://localhost:8040`, the local app on part A's code). They never play
from the live app.

## Commands (type, then Enter = `<GO>`)

| Command | Does |
|---|---|
| `NAME` | Any surname or name opens the player, e.g. `TOURE`. Synthetic names repeat (the league has seven Toures), so the player with the most minutes opens and the namesakes appear as one-click alternatives. |
| `RANK <position> <metric>` | Sets the screener's position, adds the metric as a column and sorts by it (lowest first where lower is better). The position is optional (`ALL`). |
| `CMP <player> <player> [<player>]` | Sets the compare set. The radar overlays the players and a table shows them side by side. Names can be full, surnames, or separated by commas. |
| `SCAT <metricX> <metricY> [<position>]` | The scatter. Metrics can be keys or labels (`SCAT progressive passes key passes MF` works). |
| `SIM`, `SHOT`, `FORM`, `HEAT`, `RDR <player>` | Focuses the player and opens that panel. With no player, they use the focus player. |
| `TEAM <club>` | Team style. The club can be a short name, a name, or the start of one (`TEAM brook`). |
| `HELP` | Commands, keys, linking, the numbers' rules and every source, with links. |

Shortcuts: a club's short name alone (`BRO`) opens TEAM, and a metric alone (`goals`) runs `RANK ALL goals`.

**Autocomplete** offers commands, players, clubs, metric keys and labels, and positions, depending on where you are
in the command. Metric names match word by word, so `aerial win %` finds `aerial_win_pct`. Up and Down move through
the suggestions and Tab takes one. A player or club at the top level runs at once.

**History:** Up and Down when no suggestion is highlighted (the last 50 are kept with the workspace).
**Cmd+K or Ctrl+K** focuses the command line from anywhere in the sandbox and opens the Terminal.
**Escape** closes the suggestions, then clears the line. It also closes help and tooltips.
**Alt+1 to Alt+9** jump to panels F1 to F9.

## Panels (function-key row F1 to F9; each can be hidden, moved earlier or later, and sized S, M or L)

- **F1 SCRN Screener.** A sortable table with position filters, a name or club filter, a "qualified" toggle and a
  Value or Pctl display. "Add column" lists all 127 metrics by group; × removes a column. Shading is the percentile
  against position peers: yellow for judged metrics, blue-grey for style markers. Players below the minimum for a
  percentile show in grey italics. The table has one tab stop: arrow keys, Home, End, PageUp and PageDown move, and
  Enter opens. It scrolls inside its own panel, and the player column stays put.
- **F2 PLYR Player.** The shirt, public bio, minutes, appearances and qualified flag, the six best percentiles for the
  position, and **SIM**: the five most similar players with similarity bars. Their shared-strength chips open that
  metric for that player.
- **F3 RDR Radar vs peers.** One player gives a pizza chart of the position template (or "All-round"), coloured by
  group, with a table under it. With a compare set it becomes a radar overlay plus a side-by-side table.
- **F4 CLIP Clips.** A video player, prev/next, "Play all", and the list of moments (round, opponent, minute, the
  event's value and note). A list capped at 10 shows "First 10 of N" and links to the full list on the local app. If a
  clip fails to load, the panel says to start the local app.
- **F5 SHOT Shot map.** A half pitch with shots sized by xG and coloured by outcome. Each shot is focusable and plays
  its clip; the clips panel then holds all of the player's shots. Above the map: shots, goals, npxG, npxG per shot and
  shots on target.
- **F6 HEAT Heat map.** The 12 x 8 touch grid, attacking left to right. The cells have no clips, as the panel says;
  the touches, touches in the box and progressive passes received above it open their moments.
- **F7 FORM Form.** Bars per match for any metric, with the season value as a dashed line. Each bar opens that match's
  moments. Below: the consistency table (mean, SD, CV, last three minus season) for the position's default columns.
- **F8 SCAT Scatter.** X and Y metric pickers, a position filter, "include unqualified", and median lines. Click a dot
  to select the player. The chart is one tab stop: arrow keys step through players and Enter selects. A readout shows
  the player's two values as evidence buttons.
- **F9 TEAM Team style.** A club switcher; the 19 team metrics with value, rank of 6, league average and a range bar;
  the squad by minutes, where each name selects the player.

## How linking works

There is one **focus** player and a **compare** set of up to 3; both are shown in the strip above the panels.
Selecting a player anywhere sets the focus and the team panel follows their club. That covers screener names,
screener numbers, scatter dots, the scatter readout, similar players, the squad list, radar legends and the CMP chips.
Every panel then repaints, keeping keyboard focus and scroll position. `+ Compare` adds the focus to the compare set.
The radar shows the compare set when it has two or more players. The screener marks compared rows with their colour,
and the scatter colours and labels them.

**Every number opens the moments behind it.** Each value is a button. It loads the engine's evidence into CLIP, and
the first clip plays from `app + video_url`:
- Event metrics list up to 10 moments, using the indexes into the player's `moments`.
- Physical metrics list one value per match, with no clip, and the panel says so.
- Team numbers list their per-match values. Set-piece xG lists its shots, matched by `match_id` and `event_id` to the
  taker's shot, so each one plays its clip.

**Tooltips:** every metric label, header and value shows its definition, rule, source titles, unit, whether higher is
better, the noisy flag with its split-half r, the minimum attempts, and the player's value with count and percentile.
They appear on hover and on keyboard focus, through `aria-describedby`.

## Workspaces

There are three workspaces (WS 1, 2, 3) plus Reset. Each holds the panel order, sizes and visibility, the focus and
compare players, the screener's position, columns, sort, filter and mode, the scatter axes, the form metric and the
radar template. They are kept in `localStorage` under `regista.terminal.v1`, and every read and write is in
try/catch. When storage is blocked, the workspaces live in memory for the visit and the page says "not saved: storage
blocked". A saved workspace is validated on load: unknown players, metrics and panels are dropped.

## Checks (served on 127.0.0.1:8171, local app on :8040 running part A's code)

- No console errors at 1440 x 900 or 375 x 812. The page never scrolls sideways (`scrollWidth` equals the viewport
  width at both); wide tables scroll inside their panels.
- Clips played from the local app (`readyState` 4) in three cases: a screener number (Toure, npxG, "First 10 of 19"),
  a shot on the shot map, and a set-piece xG item in TEAM.
- Every command above was run, including ambiguous names, wrong input (an error with suggestions), metric labels, and
  HELP opening and Escape closing it with focus returned.
- Keyboard checks: arrows in the screener and the scatter, Enter on SVG buttons, Cmd+K, history, and Escape on
  tooltips.
- Panel controls: hide (the F-key shows it as off and takes focus), show, size, move (saved), and Reset.
- Blocked storage was checked with a throwaway page whose `localStorage` getter throws; the page was deleted
  afterwards.
- The terminal's DOM holds no hidden-rating keys. The export script already asserts that `terminal.json` has none.

## Open

- Evidence is capped at 10 per number in `terminal.json`. The full list is a link to the local app's JSON, because
  the app sends no CORS headers, so the sandbox cannot fetch it.
- When a name is ambiguous, the regular (most minutes) opens. `USTINOV` opens Otis (301 min) rather than Amari
  (270 min), and the alternatives are one click away.
- Heat-map cells and form consistency SD and CV are not evidence buttons, because the export has no moments behind
  them.
