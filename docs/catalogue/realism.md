# Catalogue realism report

Generated by `python -m regista.synth.report --catalogue --out docs/catalogue/realism.md`; do not edit by hand. Everything in it is synthetic: Regista's own simulated players, measured exactly, set against what published studies report. Below the professional line almost nothing is published, so each level gets only the checks a source supports, and says so.

**Measurement:** the committed file `data/catalogue/realism.json`, its checks re-run on its stored aggregates. Generator 0.7.1, catalogue fingerprint `3a2d69dde65217a6`, 6 regenerated seasons of every competition's full double round robin (1,524 matches), never the 3-8 uploaded matches, which are far too few for a verdict.

Absolute values at every level sit on a Premier-League-calibrated scale (the Regista League was tuned to Premier League bands and every level is scaled from it), so they likely overstate real football below the professional line: read numbers within their pools.

## Kinds of check

- **band**: a published band read for this level: pass or fail.
- **order**: a direction a source reports between levels, checked on the pooled seasons. A step passes at 2 standard errors or more the way the source reports, fails as clearly the other way, and is otherwise not separated. Where the source also gives a size (the women's pass gap), the step must fall inside it.
- **assumed order**: a direction no source covers at these levels (Regista's assumption). Measured the same way, never counted.
- **reference**: a figure from a higher level shown beside the number, no verdict.
- **none**: no published band. The number is shown; nothing is claimed.

## Speed zones

- Women's pools: Speed zones are the men's FIFA zones (20 and 25 km/h) at every level; women's tournaments are analysed with 19 and 23 km/h [S11], so these women's numbers are lower than on women's zones and many players have none: compare them within women's pools only.
- Youth pools: Speed zones are the men's senior FIFA zones (20 and 25 km/h) at every level; youth studies use lower or individual thresholds (very high intensity above 16.1 km/h in [S8]) and U17 match peaks average 26.8 km/h [S5], so few academy players reach 25 km/h and many have no sprints: compare these numbers within youth pools only.

## Not checked

- women's second-half drop in high-intensity distance [S1]: the engine's metrics do not split halves
- Long-ball share, or any 'more direct' style, by level: no source was found.
- Event rates of any single level against a band: none is published below the professional line.
- Each competition's uploaded matches (3-8): far too few for a verdict. The checks run on the levels' full regenerated seasons.

## Method

level aggregates pooled over every competition of the level and every measurement season (regista.catalogue.measure: each competition's full double round robin regenerated 6 times). A band passes when the pooled value is inside it. An order step's margin is the higher level's value minus the lower's; its standard error combines the two levels' per-season standard errors; the step passes at 2 standard errors or more the way the source reports, fails as clearly the other way, and is otherwise not separated.

Every competition is classified on tier, age group and gender; its level is the pool key `tier/age_group/gender`. Competitions of one level are pooled: Semi-pro men: Regista League, Weserland-Liga; College men: Atlantic Collegiate Soccer Conference, Minato Bay University League; Amateur men: Regional Preferente Levante Interior, Ogun Riverside Amateur League, Liga Amateur del Valle Alto; Academy U20: Campeonato Serrano Sub-20; Academy U19: Liga Juvenil Levante Interior, Ligue Juniors Atlas Centre U19; Academy U18: Shires Academy League U18; Academy U17: Ashanti Colts Academy League U17; Semi-pro women: Pennine Women's League; College women: Pacific Coast Collegiate Conference.
